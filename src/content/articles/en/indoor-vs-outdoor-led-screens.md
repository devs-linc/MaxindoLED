Not all LED screens are created equal. One of the most common mistakes when renting is treating indoor and outdoor screens as interchangeable, when in fact they are engineered for very different conditions. Choosing the wrong type can leave your image washed out in sunlight or your panels damaged by rain. This article explains the key differences.

## Brightness Differences

The biggest differentiator is brightness, measured in nits. Outdoor LED screens need 5,000 to 8,000 nits to stay clearly legible under direct sunlight. Indoor screens, by contrast, only need 800 to 1,500 nits because room lighting is controlled. Using an indoor screen outdoors during the day will make the image look pale and nearly unreadable.

## Weather Resistance

Outdoor panels carry a high IP (Ingress Protection) rating, usually IP65 or above, making them resistant to rain, dust, and humidity. Indoor panels are not designed for this and can be damaged by water. If your event is outdoors without a roof, weather resistance is an absolute requirement that cannot be compromised.

## Pixel Pitch and Viewing Distance

Because outdoor audiences generally watch from a distance, outdoor screens often use a larger pixel pitch like P5 to P10, which is more economical. Indoor screens usually use a small pitch like P2 to P3.9 because viewers are closer and demand a smoother image.

## Structure and Weight Considerations

Outdoor panels are generally heavier and require stronger rigging and support structures to withstand wind. Make sure your venue can handle the load and that you have any permits needed for installing structures. An experienced technical crew will calculate wind load and installation safety.

## What About Semi-Outdoor Events?

Many events take place on terraces or under tents with no walls but a roof. For these conditions, consider the ambient brightness and the chance of blowing rain. Discuss with your provider to choose a panel with the right brightness and protection level.

## Conclusion

Choosing between indoor and outdoor LED screens depends on location, ambient lighting, and weather conditions. Picking wrong is not only a matter of appearance but also safety and cost.
