A wedding is one of the most precious moments in life, and the quality of the audio visual setup largely determines how smoothly it runs. Clear MC audio, well-timed music, an LED screen showing beautiful moments, and romantic lighting all leave a lasting impression on your guests. Here is a guide to planning your wedding audio visual needs from the ground up.

## Start with Guest Count and Venue

The first step is understanding the scale of your event. The guest count determines the sound system capacity, while the venue size determines the LED screen dimensions. An indoor wedding for 200 guests has very different needs than an 800-guest outdoor reception. Draw a simple floor plan marking the stage, guest seating, and MC position so your AV provider can calculate requirements accurately.

## A Balanced Sound System

A wedding sound system has to handle two things at once: vocal clarity for the MC and ceremony, plus background music and live bands. Make sure there are backup microphones, both wireless and headset, for important moments like the vows. Even speaker placement prevents some guest areas from being too loud or too quiet.

## LED Screen for Memorable Moments

The LED screen has become an essential element at many modern weddings. The screen can show a live feed of the ceremony, prewedding videos, greetings from distant relatives, and thank-you messages. For weddings, choose a small pixel pitch like P2.5 or P3 because guests are often close to the stage and the screen.

## Lighting That Sets the Mood

Lighting is not just illumination; it shapes the atmosphere. Use warm light for a cozy, romantic feel, paired with uplighting on the decor and a spotlight on the couple as they enter. Coordinate the lighting with the decoration team so the colors and intensity align beautifully.

## Coordinate with Other Vendors

Audio visual does not stand alone. Schedule coordination with the wedding organizer, decoration, and catering teams so load-in and setup schedules do not clash. Allow at least a few hours for a technical rehearsal before guests arrive to confirm everything works.
